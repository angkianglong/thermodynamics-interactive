How Does the Universal Gas Constant R̄ Emerge?

Hold one gas at a fixed temperature, visit several equilibrium pressures, calculate p v̄/T, then extrapolate the measured trend toward p → 0.

1 · Isothermal piston experiment

Choose a temperature, then move the piston. Compression changes p and molar volume v̄, while T remains constant.

2 · Plot p v̄/T versus pressure

Start: record several measurements at different pressures. At finite pressure, a real gas need not give exactly R̄.

3 · What the experiment proves

limp→0 (p v̄ / T) = R̄
The curves can differ at finite pressure. But when extrapolated to zero pressure, all temperatures approach the same limiting value. Repeating the procedure for other gases gives the same limit. That common limit is the universal gas constant, R̄ = 8.314 kJ/(kmol·K).
